# The language

An interface in linen is paper, hairlines and one ink. The content is the loudest thing on a page; the chrome around it steps back. `src/styles/linen.css` holds every token below, and `src/motion/tokens.ts` every duration. A component that states a colour, type size, radius or duration instead of naming a token is wrong; spacing comes from the 4px scale, and a 2px step only centres words, sets an icon or insets what is pressed inside a control or a framed box, never spaces parts. The specimen (`pnpm specimen`) draws all of it with the components themselves.

## Colour

Every neutral takes the app's `--hue` at a chroma of 0.02 or less, so the page is one temperature. Tints are mixes of an ink into the surface, never colours picked by eye. Each colour is one `light-dark()` pair; the page follows the system scheme, and `data-theme` on the root pins either.

| Token                                       | Marks                                                                                                   |
| ------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `ink`, `accent`                             | What a hand can act on or has set: the primary action, links, focus, the current place, a chosen option |
| `accent-weak`                               | The ink mixed into the surface: a choice under the pointer or keys, the current place's fill, a notice  |
| `warn`                                      | A state that wants attention, and nothing else                                                          |
| `danger`                                    | Removal and failure                                                                                     |
| `positive`                                  | Something gained or arrived                                                                             |
| `positive-weak`, `warn-weak`, `danger-weak` | Each state's ink mixed into the surface, as `accent-weak` is: the ground of a message in that state     |
| `bg`, `surface`, `sunken`                   | The page, a card on it, a band below the page (the rail, a well, a form opened inside a card)           |
| `border`, `border-strong`                   | Hairlines between surfaces; the frame of a control                                                      |
| `text`, `text-secondary`, `text-muted`      | The thing; what it means; text about it (labels, units, times)                                          |

Depth is tone. Nothing at rest casts a shadow; `shadow-overlay` belongs to what floats over the page: popups, dialogs, toasts and the rail's tips.

## Type

| Voice   | Face             | Speaks for                                                 |
| ------- | ---------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| Display | `--font-display` | The wordmark and a screen's title                          |
| Working | `--font-sans`    | Everything else                                            |
| Mono    | `--font-mono`    | What is typed or copied exactly: addresses, commands, keys |

Hierarchy comes from weight and colour before size: a step down in importance moves from `text` to `text-secondary` to `text-muted` first. Everything is in sentence case. Figures read in columns are tabular.

## Layout

linen draws for four screens, and each is a first-class target: a page is right only once it is right on all four. Sizes are CSS pixels, and each screen is checked at the sizes it names.

| ---------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Phone            | Tall and narrow, 360 to 414 wide                                                                              | 360 × 800, 414 × 896                       | Below `md`: the places in a bar, one column                       |
| Foldable, folded | A phone's width, 416 to 466, on a short screen                                                                | 416 × 657, 466 × 678                       | As a phone                                                        |
| Foldable, open   | Landscape and short, 816 to 890 wide and about 620 tall, and the same turned upright; some open nearly square | 816 × 616, 890 × 626, 616 × 816, 645 × 715 | From `md` (768): the rail beside the screen; below it, as a phone |
| Desk             | A laptop or a monitor, 1366 to 1920 wide, less the height the browser's own bars take                         | 1366 × 768, 1920 × 1080                    | The rail beside the screen                                        |

A short window is as much a target as a narrow one. An open foldable gives the rail and a desk's frame about 620 of height, so every dialog keeps its title and actions on screen with its body scrolling between them (`DialogLayout`), and sticky bars leave the content room.

Anything that floats at the foot of a phone's screen, as a toast does, stands `--inset-tabbar` above the edge: the tab bar's height while one is on the page, nothing otherwise. The frame starts below `--inset-top`, the band a translucent status bar covers in an app saved to a phone's home screen: the rail and the phone's top bar reach up under it in their own colour, and everything sticky sticks below it. A screen outside the shell keeps clear of it itself. Whatever a screen scrolls to, by a link to it, a step or the focus, comes to rest at the foot of the bars stuck above it, never under them; the bars, and the popups over them, move nothing as the focus passes through them. A box that scrolls inside a screen takes that margin off what it scrolls (`**:scroll-mt-0`), so its rows come to rest at its own edge.

Every card's contents start on `--spacing-gutter` (12px, 16px from `md`). Under its head a card is a stack of bands, rows, a body, a form band, a note or `Nothing`, with a hairline across the card between one and the next, and every band holds its contents a gutter from each of its edges, so a control at a band's end stands as far from its top as from its side; a row an app builds for a list in a card wears `BAND_CLASS`. An `EmptyState` centres its words in a card of its own, 48px from its top and bottom. A list inside a band, such as `Facts`, parts its rows with hairlines that end on the gutter, 8px from the row on either side.

Between the parts inside a band, space takes one of four steps. A description stands straight under what it describes. A name stands 4px over the control or box it names, and a hint, an error or a refusal 4px under it. Parts that stand together as one run are 8px apart: controls side by side, a title and its count, a value and its copy control, a gauge's name, bar and value, an icon and its words, a name and the group under it, and a control that wraps under its words. Where words take the room and what stands beside them ends the line, as a setting's control, a head's actions, a fact's value, a notice's action, a log's controls and the figure beside a progress's phase do, the two keep 16px between them; and things stacked one under another, fields, blocks, groups and the actions that end a form, stand 16px apart, which `CardBody` and `FormBand` give each child, one block each. Fields side by side in a row are things of their own too, and stand 16px apart across it, the room they keep once the row stacks them. A name inside a card, of a setting, a field, a log or a group, is `text-sm` in `font-medium`; only the card's title is larger. Words that describe something keep one reading measure, `max-w-prose`. A screen's content stands as far from its bars as from the screen's sides, and `Content` stacks its cards 16px apart, as a grid of cards stands.

Two settings never share a line. A setting's control stands at the end of its row while it fits beside the words, and under them once it does not, where it starts the line and fills it if it grows, as a head's actions do. Actions sit beside a card's title while the title and its count keep their own width beside them, and under its description once they would not, where they take the whole line, so a control among them that grows fills it. The title stands in a row a control tall whether actions stand beside it or not, so heads side by side or one under another keep their titles, descriptions and hairlines in line. A form in a card ends with its actions 16px under it, at the end of the line with the primary last.

## Controls

| Height token               | Height | Used by                                      |
| -------------------------- | ------ | -------------------------------------------- |
| `--height-control`         | 32px   | Buttons, fields, pickers, segmented controls |
| `--height-control-compact` | 28px   | Filters and bar chips                        |
| `--height-control-inline`  | 24px   | Inline edits and row chips                   |

- A button wears a frame and a fill, never words alone. One primary per card, form or dialog. A destructive button sets its words in `danger` inside the same frame and opens a confirmation. A link that starts an action wears `buttonClass()`; a link in running text wears `LINK_CLASS`.
- A field wears the same frame everywhere, its name above it and its hint below. Its value starts at the frame's inset, where every field's value starts, on the line every field's words stand on; a unit follows the value a space after it, inside the frame, in `text-muted`. A field taking no input dims to half, its frame and all it holds at once. Its `className` dresses the control's frame: how wide the control is, is its `width`, and where the field stands is what holds it.
- A field keeps no room around itself, so it stands right in a stack that spaces by a gap and in one that spaces by margins. What holds fields stands them as the Layout rule says, as `CardBody`, `FormBand` and a dialog's body do; an element of the app's own that stacks fields says `flex flex-col gap-4` or `grid gap-4`.
- A picker ends in one chevron, which turns while its list is open. Its list is never wider than the screen beside it, and a long choice wraps. A list of more than one kind of thing holds each kind under its heading. A field's stated width is its control's, and gives way to a narrower row. A narrow field's name and messages may be wider than its control: they take the room the field stands in, its cell in a grid or column, and in a row of fields the field widens to hold them, so a refusal reads in a line. A row of fields keeps its controls on one line, and a message hangs below it. In a column narrower than 28rem, a phone's, the row stands its fields one under another, each filling the column whatever width it states, and its actions under them.
- A setting that takes effect as it turns is a `Switch`; a choice a form submits later is a `Checkbox`. A choice whose answers each need a line is `OptionCards`.
- One value out of a few answers of a word or two, which all fit the row, is a `SegmentedControl`. More answers, or longer ones, are a `Select`, and a list nobody can scan is a `SearchSelect`.
- Making something asks in a `FormDialog`; every dialog lays out its title, description, body and actions with one `DialogLayout`, whose title names it. A dialog that goes on to show what it made, such as a code to send, keeps that layout: the title and the actions change with the view, and the body swaps in place with a `stepped` `Swap`: each view is a step of what the person does, so the view arriving takes the focus though the act stood among the actions, and shows from its start. A step whose act goes through a dialog or a menu, such as a confirmation, hands the focus on the same way once that has gone, unless the person has put it elsewhere. A swap under a control that stays, such as a chart and its numbers, leaves the focus on the control.
- A dialog stands in the part of the window a person can see, an on-screen keyboard's room taken out, and never grows past it. In a window too short for all of it, the title and the actions keep their places and what lies between them scrolls, with a hairline at each edge something lies past, so every field stays within reach.
- A dialog's confirm that starts something taking a moment returns its promise, and the dialog waits until it settles, kept or failed: the confirm is busy, and neither Cancel, Escape nor a press beside it leaves. The app bounds the wait, so an answer that never comes ends as a failure the dialog says. No dialog closes itself on confirm; the app closes it once the thing is done, or keeps it open with the error that says why not.
- A destructive act confirms in a `ConfirmDialog` that says what it does, an alert that a press beside it does not dismiss. An act that can be put back exactly takes a toast's undo instead.
- Work a person keeps with a save button is a draft: `useUnsavedChanges` reports it to the one `UnsavedChangesGuard` around the pages, which holds the app's moves through its navigation's `useBlocker` and asks before leaving a page that holds unsaved drafts, names them, and lets the browser ask before the tab closes. A setting that takes effect as it turns is no draft.
- A toast is Base UI's: it stands over every dialog and answers a press without closing it. F6 takes the keyboard to it and Shift+Tab back, its time rests while a pointer or the keyboard is on it, and a swipe or Escape sends it away.
- A control that starts something asynchronous is `busy` until the answer comes: a pass of light crosses its face, it keeps its words and its width, and it takes no second press. A switch that writes as it turns holds the same way.
- A field whose value was refused says why under the control, before its hint, in `danger`; the control is marked invalid and described by both.
- A refused submit takes the focus to the first field it refused, each time it is refused, so its name and its error are read together. A `Form`, and with it every form dialog, does this once the answer has come: at once, or when the promise its submit returned settles. A submit before then starts nothing. A field's own check (`validate`) refuses before the app is asked; the app's answer refuses through the form's `errors`, by each field's `name`. The app sets `errors` from every answer, nothing once nothing was refused, and never clears them as a submit starts. Each error stands until its value is edited, and the form refuses a submit at once until then, so a field's error says only what is wrong with its value; a refusal for any other reason is a `Notice`.
- The state something is in is a `Status`: a dot in the tone and a word or two. Words that want attention, a warning or a failure, wear the ink too, and something happening now beats. A state the person cannot change, such as access that only reads, is an `ExplainedStatus`, which says why in its `description`: its words, underlined in dots, open it under a resting pointer, at a press or at a key, and a press or a key takes the focus to it, so it is read out. A `description` that is nothing, as `false`, `null` or an empty string from a condition is, makes a plain `Status`.
- A failure, warning, note or success about an act or some fields is a `Notice` beside them, on its tone's tint, with the one thing to do about it at its end. A failure is read out at once; the others are read politely when their words change while they stand.
- A value on a bounded scale is a `Gauge`: a track, the scale; a fill, the value, in the ink of each zone it reaches; marks where the value was held or a threshold is set; and the value in words, at the size of the words around it. Only the fill is tinted, so a gauge at its lowest reads as empty, and a threshold worth seeing before the fill reaches it is a set mark. A held mark is a block on the bar in the text's ink, and draws nothing at the scale's lowest, where the value reached nothing; a set mark is a thin line that stands past the bar's edges, so the two read apart where they stand close. The value read out names each mark on the bar after it, by its label, as `-12 dB, Peak -3 dB` does. Gauges read together stand in `Gauges`. In a row of a list, gauges are `compact`: their bars alone, thin, their marks inside them, their names and values read out but not shown. A value that changes many times a second is handed over as something it follows, so nothing around it renders.
- A value to take whole is a `CopyValue`: the value in a sunken well, in mono, with its copy control beside it. A secret shown once is a `ShownOnce`, which reads as a setting does: its name, then what to do with it and what losing it means, in the app's words where the default does not fit, then the secret in the same well beside a button that says what it copies. Names and values read at a glance are `Facts`: each name starts its line and its value ends it, on the edge a setting's control stands on, a hairline between one fact and the next, and a value too long to stand beside its name goes under it. A value typed exactly is set in mono and taken whole.
- A number set within a range is a `NumberField`: it moves in steps, a large step with Shift held, ten of its steps unless it says otherwise, and to either end with Home and End. Its step down and step up stand together at the frame's end, where a picker's chevron stands, and at an end of the range only the step that cannot go further dims. It hands on each step and keystroke in `onValueChange` and the value once it settles in `onValueCommitted`, Base UI's names. At the end of a setting's row, where the row names it, it is a bare `CellNumber`.
- Something under way in place is a `Progress`: its phase in words over a bar that fills, or, where it cannot tell how far, a stretch that travels along the bar.
- A task of known steps shows them with `Steps`: each done, the one under way and the ones to come. Back, Cancel and the next step are its buttons, and it may open at any step, the ones before it done.
- Text a machine writes is a `LogView`: monospace in a bounded box that follows new lines until the reader scrolls up, copied or saved whole.

## Motion

Motion says what changed and where it went, and never holds input.

- A change applies at once and the animation follows it. Anything leaving is inert from its first frame.
- Every frame is true: an interrupted animation turns from where it stands.
- Only opacity and transforms move, except a height that is itself the change (a panel unfolding, a view swapping).
- `prefers-reduced-motion` makes every duration zero and leaves loops at rest, read live.
- Motion moves between states and never holds one. A state a person reads, such as a tip, the direction of a fold, a mark, a fill or a turn, is the stylesheet's, so an app without Motion keeps every state and every motion CSS runs.

Durations are springs with no bounce; a component names a purpose, never a length. `motion.css` holds the first three as `--motion-leave`, `--motion-state` and `--motion-enter`, and the holds and loops as `--motion-mark`, `--motion-land`, `--motion-thinking` and `--motion-live`; a test holds them to `src/motion/tokens.ts`.

| Token      | Length | For                                                                                              |
| ---------- | ------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `leave`    | 120ms  | Something on its way out                                                                         |
| `state`    | 180ms  | A mark sliding, a control changing, a panel folding                                              |
| `enter`    | 280ms  | An arrival, or travel to a new place                                                             |
| `stagger`  | 80ms   | The step between related arrivals                                                                |
| `mark`     | 1200ms | A new row's tint fading                                                                          |
| `land`     | 1000ms | A refresh indicator's turn                                                                       |
| `thinking` | 1500ms | One pass of light across a control waiting on an answer, or along a bar that cannot tell how far |
| `live`     | 2000ms | One beat of something happening now                                                              |

| Pattern                | Component                                             | Motion                                                                                                                                                                 |
| ---------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| A screen changes       | `Shell`                                               | The next screen fades in over `enter` once it has drawn                                                                                                                |
| A popup opens          | `POPUP_CLASS`                                         | Grows from its control's edge over `enter`, leaves toward it over `leave`                                                                                              |
| A dialog opens         | `DialogRoot`, `DialogShell`                           | Rises in the middle with its scrim over `enter`, keeps its words while it leaves                                                                                       |
| A panel unfolds        | `DisclosurePanel`                                     | Travels to its height over `enter`, folds over `state`                                                                                                                 |
| A view swaps           | `Swap`                                                | Travels between heights over `enter` while the views cross-fade; the view arriving takes the focus the one leaving held, or that a step's act left (`stepped`)         |
| A choice moves         | `SegmentedControl`, `SectionNav`, the phone's top bar | The chosen mark slides over `state`, beneath the options it passes; in the top bar the places make room over `state`, then the chosen one's words fade in over `enter` |
| A toast comes and goes | `ToastProvider`, `useToast`                           | Arrives over `enter`, fades over `leave`; follows a swipe and leaves the way it was swiped                                                                             |
| A list changes         | `SettlingRows`                                        | Rows close up over `enter`; an arrival wears `accent-weak` for `mark`. A list takes its `scope` once its rows have loaded, so none of them reads as an arrival         |
| A control waits        | `Button` and `Switch` with `busy`                     | One pass of light across its face each `thinking`; still, the light rests across its middle                                                                            |
| Something is live      | `Status` with `active`                                | A ring leaves its dot and fades each `live`; still, the ring stays drawn                                                                                               |
| Something runs         | A condition with `active`                             | Its mark turns once each `land`                                                                                                                                        |
| How far is not known   | `Progress` without a value                            | A stretch travels along the bar each `thinking`; still, the bar fills faintly                                                                                          |
| Something arrives      | `arrive()`                                            | Settles once with a 4px rise over `enter`, `stagger` apart                                                                                                             |
| The theme changes      | `createThemeStore`                                    | One cross-fade of the whole page                                                                                                                                       |

Typing, hovering, focusing, sorting and filtering stay instant: they are the reader's own acts, repeated many times a minute.

### The frame budget

At 120 Hz a frame has 8.3ms. An animation the compositor runs is smooth whatever the main thread does; one that needs the main thread is smooth only while nothing else runs there.

- An animation never re-renders React on each frame. A component that observes its own size observes only what it lays out.
- What costs a render happens before the motion starts: a screen change draws the next screen while the old one is still shown, and only then fades it in.
- Layout projection (Motion's `layout` and `layoutId`) moves small things: a chosen mark, the places of the phone's top bar and the rows of a list. Scroll containers declare `layoutScroll`, so scrolling is not mistaken for a row edit, and a new list scope remounts its rows without travel or arrival marks.
- Reduced motion also turns off row and shared-element projection, and the theme changes without its cross-fade.

## One mechanism for each job

A component does each of these jobs the one way linen does it, so a new component reads like the old ones and a change to a mechanism reaches all of them.

| Job                                                                         | Mechanism                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| What a widget does for the hand and the keyboard: focus, keys, dismissal    | Base UI's part for it, which linen styles and never re-implements                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| A control's name, what describes it, and why its value was refused          | Base UI's field, which every field and setting's row stands on and every Base UI control inside reads; an option of a group takes `aria-labelledby` on its own words, which win over the field's name the group takes, and a box with its words is named by them the same way; a control with no name in sight takes `aria-label`                             |

| A state that shows or turns: a press, a tip, a fold, a fill, a mark, a loop | CSS in `src/styles/motion.css`, on the same springs                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                           |

## Every action shows its end state

A control answers rest, hover, focus, active and disabled in its own body, and busy wherever it starts something asynchronous. Under the pointer it steps toward its words' ink: a button's fill takes a layer of that ink, deeper under a press, a frame's hairline turns `text-muted`, quiet words turn `text` and a row of navigation fills with `border`; a choice in a list or a set of cards that the pointer or the keys are on wears `accent-weak`. Disabled, a control stands at half its ink (`dimmed`) under the pointer's arrow and answers no hover or press. A write lands somewhere visible, a failure names what failed and offers the retry where the fault is, and a state a person can enter but cannot see or leave is a defect.
